Commercial Cleaning Costs in Savage: Square Foot Pricing

What facility managers in the Savage area are paying for commercial cleaning, and what drives the number up or down.

The most common question a facility manager asks before signing a cleaning contract is: what should I expect to pay? The honest answer is that pricing in the Savage and South Metro market is more variable than any national per-square-foot average suggests. This article explains the variables and gives you the framework to evaluate a quote you receive, whether it is from us or from a competitor.

The Variables That Drive Price

Commercial cleaning contracts are priced on a combination of factors, and understanding them makes you a better buyer.

Square footage: The primary driver. But the relationship between square footage and price is not linear. A 10,000 sq ft facility with one restroom and an open floor plan cleans faster than a 10,000 sq ft facility with 12 private offices, four restrooms, and two kitchens. Square footage gives you a starting point, not a final number.

Floor surface type: Carpeted spaces require vacuuming. Hard surface spaces require mopping. Light industrial spaces with sealed concrete require more aggressive scrubbing and may require the cleaning window to align with shift changes. A facility with mixed surfaces across different areas prices differently than one with consistent coverage throughout.

Restroom count: Restrooms require more time per square foot than any other zone in a building. A facility with six restrooms and 6,000 sq ft of office will price meaningfully higher than one with two restrooms and the same office area.

Cleaning frequency: Five-night-per-week service costs more per month than three-night service, but the per-visit rate is typically lower on a higher-frequency contract because crew routing becomes more efficient. A facility that tries to economize by reducing frequency sometimes ends up with a higher per-visit cost rather than proportional savings.

Market labor conditions: The South Metro has a tight labor market for cleaning workers. The I-35 and Highway 169 corridors attract a range of employers competing for the same labor pool. A contractor pricing a Savage or Shakopee account has to pay market wage to retain crew, and that wage is different from what a Minneapolis contractor might show on a national pricing comparison.

What the South Metro Market Looks Like

Without disclosing client-specific rates, we can describe the range of contract structures we see in the Savage area. Small offices under 3,000 sq ft typically negotiate a fixed monthly rate that reflects two to three service nights per week. Mid-size offices between 5,000 and 15,000 sq ft on five-night programs are the most common contract type in this market. Light industrial and mixed-use facilities in the 15,000 to 50,000 sq ft range typically require separate pricing for office and production zones, which results in a different per-square-foot blended rate than a comparable pure office building.

Floor Care Pricing

Floor stripping and waxing is not included in base cleaning contracts—it is priced separately and charged per visit or on an annual contract basis. The cost per square foot for a strip-and-wax service on VCT varies by current floor condition (a floor that has not been stripped in three years takes longer than one on a regular cycle), the number of coats applied, and the required dry time and follow-up buffing. For most South Metro VCT floors, a quarterly strip-and-wax program and monthly buffing is the economical maintenance choice compared to a less-frequent strip cycle with higher labor cost per visit.

How to Evaluate a Quote

A cleaning quote that does not include a written scope of work is not a quote—it is a number. Before you accept any quote, confirm that the scope defines the specific tasks, the frequency for each task, the cleaning schedule, and the response protocol for service failures. A quote without a scope lets the contractor define what "commercial cleaning" means after you have signed. The scope is what makes the quote binding.
