Commercial Cleaning Contracts: What You're Actually Paying For

The details buried in a cleaning services agreement determine what you actually receive.

Signing a commercial cleaning contract can feel like a formality, but the details buried in those pages determine what you actually receive — and what you don't. Before you put pen to paper, here's what facility managers in Savage and the South Metro should understand about cleaning service agreements.

The Scope of Work Section Is Everything

The most important part of any commercial cleaning contract is the scope of work. This section should detail every task the cleaning crew is expected to perform, how often, and in which areas of your facility. Vague language like “general cleaning” is a red flag — look for itemized task lists broken down by room or zone. See our scope of work template for what a properly itemized scope looks like.

Common Line Items in a Commercial Cleaning Contract

  • Vacuuming and mopping of all floors (specify types: carpet, tile, sealed concrete)
  • Restroom sanitization and restocking (paper, soap, bags)
  • Trash and recycling removal
  • Kitchen/breakroom cleaning
  • Surface and desk wipe-downs
  • Window and glass cleaning (interior or exterior?)
  • Floor buffing or waxing (and how often?)

What's Usually NOT Included

Most standard contracts exclude biohazard cleanup, post-construction cleaning, exterior pressure washing, deep carpet extraction, and pest control. If you expect any of these occasionally, ask for a supplemental rate schedule upfront.

Watch for These Contract Clauses

Auto-renewal clauses: Many contracts automatically renew for 12 months unless you give 30–60 days' written notice.

Price escalation clauses: Some contracts allow annual price increases tied to labor costs.

Termination fees: Leaving early may cost you several months of remaining contract value.

Credit/complaint procedures: How do you report a missed clean? What's the remedy timeline?

Short-Term vs. Long-Term Contracts

Month-to-month contracts offer flexibility but typically cost more. Annual contracts lock in pricing and are better for budget predictability. A short trial period is worth asking for if you want to test a new provider before committing long-term.

Always Negotiate

Ask about trial periods and performance guarantees. A reputable cleaning company should be willing to customize the contract to your facility's needs.
