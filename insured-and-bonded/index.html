Insured, Bonded and 22 Years Serving Savage

What our insurance and bonding coverage means for your facility, and how to request a certificate of insurance.

Before a cleaning contractor enters a commercial facility, the facility manager is responsible for verifying two things: that the contractor carries adequate general liability insurance, and that crew members are bonded. These are not courtesy checks—they determine who bears financial responsibility if property is damaged or if theft occurs during a cleaning service.

General Liability Insurance

Savage Commercial Cleaning Services carries general liability insurance appropriate for commercial cleaning operations. This coverage applies to property damage and bodily injury claims arising from our work in your facility. A certificate of insurance is included with every written quote and is available on request at any time during an active service agreement.

If your building management company, landlord, or risk department requires a specific certificate format or additional insured status, let us know during the quote process. We are familiar with the documentation requirements of property management firms operating in the South Metro and can provide the correct certificate format.

Employee Bonding

Every crew member who enters a client facility is bonded. Employee dishonesty bonding provides coverage in the event of theft by a crew member. This is a standard requirement for any commercial cleaning contractor working in offices, medical suites, or multi-tenant buildings where crew members have unsupervised access to the building after hours.

Workers’ Compensation

We carry workers’ compensation coverage for all cleaning employees. This matters to facility managers because an uninsured cleaning worker injured on your property can create liability for the building owner or property manager in certain circumstances. A workers’ compensation certificate is available alongside the general liability certificate.

22 Years in Business

A contractor who has operated continuously for 22 years in a single market has maintained insurance coverage without interruption, managed claims without losing coverage, and retained the operational structure that insurability requires. That continuity is one of the stronger signals of contractor reliability available to a facility manager who cannot audit the business directly.

The combination of adequate insurance, employee bonding, and 22 years of uninterrupted operation is the case for this contractor in plain terms. The quote form and the certificate request both take less than five minutes.
